## Advice given (priority order)
1. Make stressed syllables stand out (louder, longer, higher) and reduce unstressed ones to "uh". This is the
   main lever for both speed and pitch range.
2. Let statements fall clearly at the end.
3. Link words together (consonant to vowel, flapped T).
4. Shadowing: speak along with 20–30 s clips of American speakers, copying their rhythm and pitch.
5. A checklist of sounds French speakers often get wrong, **not verified on the recording**: American R
   (including after vowels), TH, H, short vs long vowels (ship/sheep, full/fool, /æ/ in cat), flapped T
   (water → "wadder").
